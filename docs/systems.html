Private LLM Systems

Secondary page. Papers, frameworks, libraries and hardware that use the schemes listed on the main page. A catalogue of approaches to private LLM inference, centred on CKKS. It starts with the CKKS frameworks surveyed in “SoK: Private LLM Inference using Approximate Homomorphic Encryption” (Al Badawi, Alexandru, Polyakov, Vaikuntanathan, 2026). It then lists CKKS variants, bootstrapping advances, alternative FHE schemes, interactive MPC protocols, hardware and statistical alternatives, libraries, and accelerators.

How to read this table
  • Group 0 holds the CKKS frameworks compared in the SoK. Click “More” for hardware, library, packing class and reported latency.
  • Group 1 adds 2026 frameworks that appeared after or alongside the SoK, including the first Gentry–Lee matrix-FHE Transformer (Terrazzo) and a hybrid CKKS/TFHE decoder (ROSETTA).
  • Groups 2–4 are cryptographic building blocks: CKKS variants, CKKS bootstrapping and operation advances, and other FHE schemes such as BGV, BFV, TFHE and the Gentry–Lee matrix scheme.
  • Group 5 holds interactive protocols (MPC and HE+MPC hybrids). They are often faster than pure CKKS but need many communication rounds and sometimes non-colluding servers.
  • Group 6 holds non-cryptographic or integrity-only alternatives: GPU and CPU trusted execution environments, differential privacy, split inference and zero-knowledge proofs.
  • Groups 7–8 are software libraries, compilers and hardware accelerators.
  • Packing classes from the SoK: L-ROW token/row, L-COL column/diagonal, L-CYC complex/cyclic, L-ALG algorithmic shortcuts. N-PRE means model-preserving and N-MOD means the model must be modified or retrained.
  • Latencies are as reported by each paper on different hardware and are not directly comparable.
